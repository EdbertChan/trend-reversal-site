The short version

The chart lies about timing. A reversal marker appears on the candle where the turn happened — but it isn't drawn until about 50 minutes later, once price has moved far enough to prove it was a turn. You cannot trade the price under the marker.

Traded honestly, the edge is thin and fragile. The best realistic scenario returns 40.7% over a year but drops a third of the account doing it. Remove its five best days and it goes negative.

The stop level doesn't matter. 10%, 20% and 30% stops produced identical results — none of them ever triggered. 10% of NQ is about 2,900 points; the average trade lasts two hours.

Why the numbers look like this

The marker is a receipt, not a forecast

A swing high only counts once price falls 2×ATR below it — about 50 NQ points. Until that happens, nothing distinguishes it from any other local high.

On 1 April, six candles between 22:00 and 01:00 were all local highs. One got a marker. The one that got it wasn't the highest — 00:00 made a higher high and got nothing. What separated them was what price did next.

Proof, not opinion

Feed the code data ending at 00:05, 00:30, 00:45 — no signal at 00:05. Feed it data ending 00:55 — the 00:05 signal appears.

The shape of the returns

The best live scenario has a positive mean and a negative median: you lose on most days and get rescued by a few large wins. 122 green days out of 260.

Remove the five best days from any live scenario and every one turns negative. In three of them, removing two days is enough.

What we still don't know

Fees were ruled out as the explanation for the chart/live gap (under 0.01% of it), and stops were ruled out as necessary. That is not proof that timing explains the rest.

Settling it needs matched pivot records — pivot and confirmation timestamps, both views' entries and exits. The daily files can't do it.
